Biomarkers of ASD

Author

Runxin Shen, Ryan Chang, Nic Chan, Khoi Huynh

Updated

October 30, 2026

Abstract

This project looks at the serum proteomic analysis of Hewitson et al. (2021), which identified blood biomarkers for Austism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) using protein levels in 154 boys (76 ASD, 78 typically developing). We first evaluated the preprocessing. In this part, we found that raw protein levels were strongly right skewed, and a \(\log_{10}\) transformation reduced the median skewness across the proteins from about 2.25 to 0.83, which justifies its use before \(z\)-scoring and trimming at \(\pm3\). Without trimming, we counted outlying values per subject and flagged 13 subjects as extreme outliers (9 TD, 4 ASD), though outlier counts did not differ significantly between groups (Wilcoxon rank sum test, \(p = 0.817\)). We then tested three changes to the protein selection procedure: selecting features on a training split only, expanding each method’s list from the top 10 to the top 20 proteins, and replacing the hard intersection with a rank based fuzzy intersection. Of these, the top 10 hard intersection performed best, with 6 proteins, having a test accuracy of \(0.656\) and AUROC \(0.723\). Lastly, a simpler three protein panel (DERM, IgD, and RELT) outperformed the five protein in-class baseline, with accuracy \(0.806\) versus \(0.774\) and AUROC \(0.892\) versus \(0.883\), suggesting that a small number of proteins carries most of the predictive signal.

Dataset

The data came from Hewitson et al. (2021), who used serum proteomics to identify blood biomarkers for Austim Spectrum Disorder (ASD) in young boys. Blood samples were collected from 154 boys aged 18 months to 8 years: 76 with ASD and 78 typically developing (TD) boys. The level of 1317 proteins were measured for each subject. The dataset also records each subject’s group (ASD or TD) and for boys with ASD, and ADOS severity score. In the published analysis, 192 of the proteins failed quality control, which left 1125 proteins. However, our analysis includes 1317.

Preprocessing was conducted in three steps, each applied to every protein. First, a \(log_{10}\) transformation reduced the strong right skew in the raw protein levels. Second, the log transformed values were standardized to \(z\)-scores so that proteins measured on different scales could be compared. Third, outliers were trimmed by capping the standardized values to the range [-3,3], which limits the influence of more extreme measurements on the analysis.

Summary of published analysis

This study by Hewitson aimed to identify a blood biomarker panel for Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD in adolescent boys by conducting proteomic analysis of serum samples using the SomaLogic SOMAScan platform, which measures a total of 1,125 targeted proteins. For the subjects, the group that had ASD were assessed by a psychologist using the Autism Diagnostic Observation Schedule and Autism Diagnostic Interview Revised, and the typically developing group which was used as a control was screened using the Adapative Behavior Assessment System (ADOS) second edition. After analyzing subject outliers in the data, we found that the typically developing group had more subject outliers. When comparing both groups in general, they seemed to be very similar to each other, and they interestingly had the same outlier_count median. After conducting a wilcox rank sum test to compare the groups, it suggested that these two groups are not statistically significantly different based on outlier_count. We also found that, selecting more proteins did not improve predictive performance. After data normalization and a log transformation, there were three computational strategies used for the analysis were Random Forest using Mean Decrease Gini, t tests between the two groups and a correlation approach measuring the ADOS total scores to reflect clinical severity. The top ten highest average proteins were chosen for the RF based prediction model. There were 5 core proteins common to each method identified: Dermatopontin (DERM), Immunoglobulin D (IgD), Receptor Expressed in Lymphoid Tissues (RELT), Thrombospondin-4 (TSP4), and Eukaryotic Translation Initiation Factor 4H (eIF-4H), and to maximize the predictive capability, more top highly ranked proteins were added in a stepwise optimization process to see if they contributed to the classification. The three proteins were chosen by their Gini scores and were DERM, IgD, and RELT. Narrowing down the proteins to a simpler panel increased both the classification accuracy and ROCAUC.

Findings

Impact of preprocessing and outliers

We compared the raw and \(log_{10}\) transformed distributions of four randomly selected proteins. The raw levels are strongly right skewed, with most values near the low end and a long tail of large values. After the \(log_{10}\) transformation, the distributions are roughly symmetric. This holds across all proteins: the median skewness drops from around 2.25 to around 0.83, and 96.735% of proteins are right skewed on the raw scale. Since the later steps such as t-tests, z-scores, and trimming at \(\pm3\)) work best on symmetric data, the log transformation keeps a few extreme values from dominating the analysis.

After removing the outlier trimmers, we were able to work with all the data and created a row called outlier_count to determine the number of outlier proteins each subject had, and then used the interquartile range method to determine whether a subject was an outlier based on the distribution of outlier_count. We ended up finding a total of 13 out of 154 subjects being subject outliers, which ranged from having an outlier_count of 33 all the way to 157. After analyzing these subject outliers, we found that the typically developing group had more subject outliers, as they had 9 and the autism spectrum disorder group had only 4. When comparing both groups in general, they seemed to be very similar to each other, having means in outlier_count only 4.3 outlier proteins apart, and they interestingly had the same outlier_count median of 8.5. After conducting a wilcox rank sum test to compare the groups, it yielded a high pvalue of 0.8166, suggesting that these two groups are not statistically significantly differnet based on outlier_count. This supports the claim that systemic subject outliers do not seem to be different based on groups.

Methodological variations

We examined three modifications to the original protein selection procedure to evaluate their effects on classification performance.

First, we divided the dataset into 122 training subjects and 32 testing subjects using stratified sampling. Preprocessing parameters and feature selection were determined using only the training data to prevent data leakage. We selected the top 10 proteins using multiple testing and Random Forest importance, then identified six overlapping proteins: DERM, Calcineurin, MAPK2, FSTL1, IgD, and MAPK14. A logistic regression model was fitted using these proteins and evaluated on the testing set.

Second, we increased the number of proteins selected by each method from 10 to 20 while maintaining the hard intersection rule. This increased the final panel size from 6 to 11 proteins.

Third, we implemented a rank-based fuzzy intersection. Instead of requiring proteins to appear in both Top 10 lists, we assigned membership scores based on their rankings under each selection method and selected the 10 proteins with the highest combined fuzzy scores.

Results and Discussion

The Top 10 hard intersection achieved the highest accuracy (65.6%) and AUROC (0.723). Increasing the number of selected proteins reduced accuracy to 62.5%, although sensitivity improved slightly from 50.0% to 56.2%. The fuzzy intersection achieved the same reported classification performance as the Top 20 hard intersection while using one fewer protein.

Overall, selecting more proteins did not improve predictive performance. However, the results are based on a single train-test split, and a direct comparison with the original feature selection procedure is still needed to assess the effect of preventing data leakage.

To find a simpler predictive panel, we isolated the top three proteins (DERM, IgD, and RELT) from the baseline five-protein consensus set based on their Random Forest Gini impurity scores and evaluated them via logistic regression on the same train/test split. Despite a reduction in panel size, the three-protein model achieved higher classification performance and ROCAUC compared to the in-class baseline, demonstrating that the secondary markers (eIF-4H and TSP4) contributed mostly redundant variance. The baseline proteins had a ROCAUC of 0.883 and an accuracy of 0.774 while the simple panel of proteins had a ROCAU of 0.892 and an accuracy of 0.806.
